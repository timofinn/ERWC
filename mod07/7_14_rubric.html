7.14  |  Graduation Speech

Rubric

20 points total. Five categories, four points each. Check your document and ask, where does my work fall on these continuums?

Point Scale
1 Below
2 Approaching
3 Meets
4 Exceeds
Rubric
Category Below — 1 Approaching — 2 Meets — 3 Exceeds — 4
Speech Structure
4 pts
 Below — 1 One or more structural components (hook, story, significance, close) are missing or impossible to identify. Speech reads more like a list than a speech. Approaching — 2 The components are present but underdeveloped. The hook doesn't bite, or the close fizzles out. The turn from personal story to broader significance is unclear. Meets — 3 All four components are present and identifiable. The speech has a clear arc — it opens, builds, turns, and finishes clean. Structure is labeled in the Google Doc. Exceeds — 4 The hook compels. The structure is airtight and well developed. Shift from personal memory, it's significance and impact on you the speaker, and connection to life beyond high school, all crystal clear. Closer ends with a thought-provoking statement or call-to-action.
Specificity
4 pts
 Below — 1 The speech is almost entirely generalized; in other words, so generic and boilerplate, it could have been written by anyone, about anyone due to it's vagueness. No names, no scenes, no moments, just generalizations. Approaching — 2 There are attempts at specific detail, but they're surface-level. Vague references to "hard times" or "memories" without grounding them in a real moment or person. Meets — 3 At least one concrete, personal scene anchors the speech — an authentic moment, a named person, a scenic detail that rings authentic. Exceeds — 4 Multiple specific details make the speech unmistakably personal. Names, places, moments, sensory details — the kind of specificity that makes listeners lean in because they recognize something true.
Rhetorical Devices
4 pts
 Below — 1 Fewer than three devices used, or devices are present in name only — labeled but not actually executing their function in the speech. Approaching — 2 Three to four devices are present but feel forced or grafted on. The device is there, but it doesn't do anything the surrounding language wasn't already doing. Meets — 3 At least five devices are deployed and functional — they add rhythm, emphasis, or argument that the speech needs. The language is doing more work because of them. Exceeds — 4 Five or more devices are woven in naturally — they don't feel like a checklist. The speech would be noticeably weaker without them. At least one device does something surprising or genuinely memorable.
Annotation
4 pts
 Below — 1 Little to no color-coding or annotation. It's unclear which devices were used or where. No evidence of intentional craft. Approaching — 2 Some devices are highlighted but annotations are missing, incomplete, or vague ("I used anaphora here"). The rationale doesn't explain the choice. Meets — 3 All five devices are color-coded and each has a one-sentence annotation explaining why it was placed there. The student can articulate the rhetorical purpose of their choices. Exceeds — 4 Annotations are precise and show genuine craft awareness — not just "I used tricolon here" but why the tricolon at that moment, and what it does to the listener. The student sounds like a writer who made deliberate decisions.
Delivery
4 pts
 Below — 1 No video recording or live delivery submitted. Or the recording is inaudible, incomplete, or clearly a first glance at the speech with zero preparation. Approaching — 2 A recording is submitted but the delivery is entirely read off the page — no eye contact, no variation in pace or tone. The speech was read, not spoken. Meets — 3 The speech is delivered with enough presence to feel intentional. The speaker glances up, varies their pace, even pauses strategically. It sounds more like a speech than a reading. Exceeds — 4 The delivery serves the speech. Pauses are deliberate. The speaker makes the room feel the weight of the language. The performance earns the writing.
Note on delivery format: Live delivery in front of the class and video played for the class are treated equally — both demonstrate a certain amount of intenstinal fortitude to share your original work an audience, which is the point of this unit. A private demo or video submission (where you only show me) is capped at 80% of the total score, regardless of quality. The cap reflects the lowered stakes, not the effort.
